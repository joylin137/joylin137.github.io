For five years, my job was to analyse customer issues for the customer centre at a telecoms company in Taipei. I also built models to forecast how many customers would call our service centre each month, and I got good at it. 90% accurate. Every month I could tell you, almost to the day, how many people were about to be confused, frustrated or lost.

One of my responsibilities was to reduce the volume of incoming calls to cut company costs. Tackling these customer issues felt like playing whack-a-mole; as soon as one was resolved, the next popped up. According to the data, more than half the calls were from customers simply asking for their bill amount. I was measuring the cost of bad design with remarkable precision, and fixing none of it.

So I crossed to the other side of the dashboard.

I wanted to stop just tracking user frustration and start preventing it. This drove me to move to the UK to pursue an MSc in Digital Design at York, where I learned to dive deep into problems and design for real impact. My analytical roots run deep: I still don't call a design finished until the numbers agree.

Outside of work, I love travelling and hiking every trail slowly. Now, I'm looking for a UX role in the UK where I can tackle complex design challenges within a collaborative team that thrives on continuous growth. Currently based in Sheffield, I am fully flexible to relocate for the right opportunity.
